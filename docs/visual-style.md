# Balance: visual style

This is the approved look for the game. New art (tiles, units, buildings) must
match it.

## Screen and grid

- Internal resolution is **960x540**, shown at a whole-number scale (x2 gives
  1920x1080). Nearest-neighbour filtering; no smoothing.
- The grid cell is **32 px**.

## View

- Near top-down and flat, like Dune II. Almost no height anywhere; nothing looks
  tall.
- Shadows are short.

## Terrain

- Terrain is made of **flat texture patches**.
  - Forest maps: grass, dirt, stone, forest, water.
  - Desert maps: sand, dunes, rock, hardpan.
- Each patch is built from **small repeating elements** with **per-pixel grit**,
  not smooth gradients or large shapes.
- **Forest is a terrain type**, not a set of tall objects: it is a carpet of tree
  tops, each about a third of a cell across.

- The **Orichalcum rock** is a low formation of cracked rock studded with
  small gold ore crystals, flat like everything else. The control area around
  it is marked by a ring of small dots on the ground.

The terrain tiles in the game are generated by `tools/make_tiles.py`, which
reuses the palettes and the texture recipe of the v4 mockup scripts. See
[terrain.md](terrain.md).

## Colour

- A muted, natural palette.
- Warmer than a science-fiction look, but not saturated or cartoon-like.

## Units and buildings

- Soldiers are about **a third of a cell** tall.
- Vehicles are **just under one cell**.
- Every sprite has a **soft dark rim** and a **tight contact shadow**, so it sits
  on the ground. No drop shadows and no hard black outlines.
- Buildings stand **only on rock ground**, on a foundation that is flush with it.

## HUD

**Approved on 2026-11-15: mockup v12 D** ([`balance_v12_hud_d.png`](../mockups/balance_v12_hud_d.png)).
The user asked for something warmer than the old, "too serious" HUD, a top
bar from one side of the screen to the other, and rounder corners; of three
layouts they took C's look with A's heroes, message box and objectives, the
heroes stacked one above the other a little lower down.

- **Skin:** chestnut wood, gold rims, parchment panels, navy command buttons,
  rounded corners; the corner where the top bar meets the sidebar is rounded
  too. Text is Georgia (Verdana for small print), ink on parchment, light
  with a shadow on wood.
- **Top bar**, the whole width: the scales emblem, Objectives and Menu, the
  mission's name on a parchment plaque, then Orichalcum, Power and Time.
- **Heroes:** a framed portrait for each of the player's heroes down the
  top-left of the map, with a health bar and the ability's key (the seconds
  left while it recharges). Click selects, double click goes there.
- **Message box** at the bottom left of the map: what the mission's
  characters say, with their portrait, or else the hint.
- **Objectives** open as a parchment list under the Objectives button (or O).
- **Sidebar:** minimap; build tabs, a 3x2 build grid with costs, the queue
  and power (or, without a base, the objective box and the controls); a card
  about what is selected or under the mouse; the command buttons with icons.

The art is made by `tools/make_hud.py` into `assets/ui/`; the portraits there
are placeholders until the art pass (M7.6).

The screens around a mission use the same skin: the title screen (a wooden
board of buttons over a slow pan across Mission 2's map), the briefing (a
parchment dossier beside a picture of the map), the debriefing, the pause
menu and the settings board. `HudStyle` (`scripts/ui/hud_style.gd`) holds the
fonts, colours and the panels and buttons they are all built from.

## Rejected directions

| Mockup | Why it was rejected |
| --- | --- |
| v1 (`balance_mock_*`) | Dark glass science-fiction look: too cold, too technological |
| v2 | Too warm and colourful, too many round shapes, too much height and depth, soldiers bigger than trees |
| v3 | Still too cartoon-like; trees too tall and too big; the repeating elements needed to be smaller |

## A note on originality

The style borrows Dune II's flatness, proportions and readability. It does not
copy any Dune II sprite, tile, palette or screen. All art in the game is made for
this project.
